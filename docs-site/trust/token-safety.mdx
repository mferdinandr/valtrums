Valtrums checks every token before its community can play. A token must be on the allowlist, pass a security check and show enough real liquidity. Until both a fresh safety approval and a backing approval are in place, deposits for that community are held in escrow and don't count.

## The allowlist

Only five tokens can join a battle: **CASHCAT**, **WALLET**, **GOOD**, **VLAD** and **DEGEN**. Communities are always identified by chain and token contract address, never by ticker alone, so a copycat token with the same name can't join. The Valtrums DAO manages the allowlist.

## The safety check

Each community gets a fresh check that combines two independent sources.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Security scan (GoPlus)">
    Valtrums reads the token's security report from GoPlus and rejects critical risks. Under the current policy:

    - Buy, sell and transfer tax must each be **5% or less**.
    - Owner powers such as minting, pausing, blacklisting or changing balances or taxes are only allowed when ownership is **renounced and can't be taken back**.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Liquidity check">
    The token's liquidity must be **strictly above USD 10,000**, checked independently of GoPlus. Exactly USD 10,000 is not enough.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Possible results

| Result | Meaning | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pass** | All required checks passed with complete data | Deposits for that community count |
| **Pending** | Data is missing, unsupported or out of date | Deposits wait in escrow |
| **Reject** | A critical risk was found | The community can't play |

Missing data always means **Pending**, never Pass. An approval is valid for 10 minutes by default and is refreshed automatically. When a battle locks, every funded community needs a fresh approval.

<Note>
  On testnet, each test token is checked through the real Robinhood Chain token it represents. The approval describes that real token, not the test token's own contract.
</Note>

## What this doesn't promise

The check filters out known critical risks. It doesn't guarantee a token's value or future behavior, and it isn't financial advice.
