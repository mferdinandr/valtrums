# Lifecycle implementation and integration boundary

The lifecycle contract path is fail-closed. Activating it requires a fresh arena with no legacy battles and permanently rejects legacy proposals. Lifecycle representations cannot be the legacy USDC token. Existing contract state and positions are not migrated.

Creation requires payment for storage, not a proposal bond. Testnet battles are limited to 16 communities, as selected by the owner; copied metadata fields are limited to 256 bytes. A sandbox lock with 16 synthetic positions and 256-byte evidence and policy fields consumed approximately 8.01 TGas in this environment. This is a fixture measurement, not a live-network guarantee; production limits still require verification. A position must be registered before a representation transfer; the NEP-141 receiver returns a transfer that cannot be credited. A lock requires a nonempty vault for every community, a positive creator position, an observation policy, and separately recorded audit and backing evidence for every community. A locked snapshot does not change when lock is repeated, policy changes, or later transfers arrive.

Audit approvals are recorded by `submit_lifecycle_audit` (whitelisted agent) and backing approvals by `refresh_lifecycle_backing` (see below); a winner is recorded only by the oracle-driven scoring path (`submit_lifecycle_observations`). Evidence references are not proof of custody, and a `PrincipalHold` is accounting for representation units, not proof that foreign backing has been reserved or disposed. The backing, audit, scoring, settlement, and DAO changes must provide authenticated interfaces and preserve the locked position and principal invariants before live use. A Rust-only read boundary exposes each frozen losing-vault input and snapshotted fee without consulting a DAO decision; it does not allocate rewards or reserve backing. No DAO disposition or fallback is defined here.

The community asset identity carries an optional mainnet reference pair (`reference_chain_id` and `reference_address`, both or neither, validated at creation, address stored as lowercase 20-byte hex, no two communities with the same pair). It is part of asset equality, so `submit_lifecycle_audit` requires the approval's asset to equal the committed asset including the pair. This changes the borsh layout of `AssetIdentity` (and the JSON `AssetInput` and community view gain optional fields); a fresh arena is required, which lifecycle activation already demands, and no existing state is migrated. Backing, payout and receipt-registry crates define their own identity types and do not use the arena struct; only the arena (`lib.rs`, `lifecycle.rs`, `rounds_api.rs` test helpers) construct or compare it, and existing tests pass with the fields unset.

The `sandbox-fixtures` feature adds self-call-only fixture methods to a separate test build. Do not enable this feature for deployments. Fixture evidence and winners do not prove live service coverage, audit approval, actual-received backing, or scoring. Sandbox tests verify the NEP-141 receiver and local custody behavior with mock tokens; they do not establish a cross-chain deposit recovery path. Owner decision: a foreign deposit that arrives after lock never enters the snapshot or any vault; backing records it as unattributed and returns it to the original depositor after finality. That refund path belongs to the arena-asset-representation (backing) change and is not implemented here. The integration is now verified in the sandbox with the real arena and backing (`integration/tests/lifecycle_integration.rs`): a claim on a locked battle becomes a refund-only late deposit, the vault and snapshot stay unchanged and the payout authority can reserve the refund. The registry claims, custody balances and observations in that test are fixtures, not live evidence, and D17 (EVM refund address, issue #82) will change the refund destination.

The observation source, evidence freshness, cancellation, recovery, storage cleanup, and operational limits remain subject to the open decisions in the product specification. Implementation tasks remain unchecked until their full requirements, including cross-chain recovery and independent reward handling, are verified.

An audit approval carries an expiry (`expires_at_ms`, block time in milliseconds) set only by the whitelisted `submit_lifecycle_audit` pass (future, at most 24 hours ahead). `lock_lifecycle_battle` now also refuses when any community's approval is at or after its expiry, no longer equals the committed asset, or differs from the owner-pinned audit policy hash (`set_lifecycle_audit_policy_hash`, owner, one yocto, optional); a repeat lock on a locked battle returns the original snapshot without re-checking. The community view gains `audit_expires_at_ms`. The arena-audit change owns the details.

Backing approval (`lifecycle_backing.rs`). `refresh_lifecycle_backing(battle_id, community)` is agent-only, needs an attached deposit that pays storage (excess refunded) and is refused for a locked battle or a representation that is not on the owner's trust list (`set_lifecycle_representation_trusted`, owner, one yocto; `is_lifecycle_representation_trusted` view). It joins the representation's `get_asset` and `get_ledger` views and the private callback `on_lifecycle_backing_checked` stores a `BackingApproval` (new field `expires_at_ms`, now plus `BACKING_APPROVAL_MS` = 10 minutes) only when chain, token and custody domain equal the committed asset and the ledger is balanced, not paused and not under reconciliation. A mismatch, suspension or failed call withdraws the approval; an unpaid write is rolled back. The evidence hash is a SHA-256 of the representation, asset and block height, a reference and not a proof. `lock` now also requires every backing approval to equal its committed asset and be unexpired. The arena state gains `lifecycle_trusted_representations` and `StorageKey::LifecycleTrustedRepresentations`, and `BackingApproval` gains a field: both change the borsh layout, which a fresh arena already requires. Deposits remain credited in `ft_on_transfer` only from the community's representation and only after every community has audit and backing approval; a backing suspended after approval is caught by the lock-time expiry and a fresh refresh. Removing a representation from the trust list does not withdraw approvals it already produced. The lock does not re-read the representation, so a deficit that appears within the 10-minute life is not seen until the next refresh.

## Round history and application views (#144)

**Views.** `get_lifecycle_battle_v2(battle_id: U64)` returns `{schema_version: 2, battle_id, battle}` where `battle` is the unchanged v1 `LifecycleView`; `get_lifecycle_battle_count_v2() -> U64`; `get_lifecycle_position_v2(battle_id: U64, community, account)`; `get_lifecycle_readiness()` returns `{schema_version, paused, lifecycle_activated, oracle, protocol, fee_bps, creator_share_bps, observation_policy_configured}` (stored facts only; it does not prove backing, custody or that a battle can be created). v1 methods are untouched, so deployed clients keep working. Fields are only ever added within a schema version.

**Round finalization.** When a round resolves to a winner or an overtime, `Rounds` stores a `commitment` (sha256, format `valtrum-round-v1`) next to the recorded outcome, and the arena emits one `round_finalized` event (`schema: "round-finalized-v1"`): `battle_id`, `round_id`, `outcome` (`winner` or `overtime`), `winner`, `next_candidates`, `start_ns`, `end_ns`, `policy_hash`, `commitment` (hex) and `scores[]` (`community`, `evidence_hash`, `negative`, `numerator`, `denominator`, all decimal strings). A pending resolution records nothing and emits nothing; repeating a recorded round returns the recorded outcome and emits nothing. The commitment hashes, in order and length-prefixed or fixed width: the label, battle id, round id, window start and end (u64 big endian), the policy hash, the score count and, per candidate in ascending community order, the community (u32), the evidence hash, the sign byte and the 32-byte big-endian numerator and denominator. `get_lifecycle_round_commitment(battle_id, round_id)` returns it (hex) or `None`. The indexer recomputes the commitment from the event's detail and rejects an event whose detail does not match (cross-language vector test).

**What is new state versus event-only.** New state: one 32-byte commitment per finalized round (inside the stored `Record`). Event-only: the window, scores and evidence hashes. Existing fields are unchanged.

**Upgrade and storage.** The borsh layout of `Record` changes (one added field). As for the asset identity change above, lifecycle activation already requires a fresh arena with no legacy state and nothing is migrated; do not upgrade an arena that already holds finalized lifecycle rounds in place. Storage cost is 32 bytes per finalized round (about 0.00032 NEAR). `submit_lifecycle_observations` attaches only one yoctoNEAR, so this is paid from the arena account's own balance, exactly like the existing per-round history entry it extends; the arena account must stay funded for it. Nothing else gains storage.

**Backfill limits.** A round finalized before this event existed has no commitment and no event: readers show it as `incomplete` and never reconstruct a score, window or evidence for it. The event is emitted exactly once, at finalization. An indexer that did not observe that block (outside its retained or anchored range) has no detail for that round either; the commitment view still proves a round was finalized, but the scores cannot be recovered from state.

**Not exposed.** Backing and custody readiness, per-asset trust and indexer health are not arena state; readers take them from the backing contract and the indexer and keep them `unavailable` when absent.
