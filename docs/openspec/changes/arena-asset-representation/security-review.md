# Backing security review

This is an internal review, not an independent audit or evidence of live source-chain custody.

- **Critical integration gate:** `claim_receipt` is now a public method, so the registry becomes the only mint authority. Its trust rests on the registry owner and operator verifier (trusted testnet parties, not cryptographic proof of Robinhood state), on the immutable registry and mapping version fixed at initialization, and on a registry that records each `(chain_id, transaction hash, log index)` once. No live verifier account, custody deployment or real source receipt exists; sandbox tests use synthetic attestations. Do not deploy or fund this path until the operator, custody contract and token admissibility policy are reviewed.
- **Claim path:** the callback credits only the claim's beneficiary, only when the claim matches this asset, custody domain, mapping version and representation account, and only once per receipt; every other outcome refunds the deposit. A registry or operator that attests a false receipt can mint backed units for it. The 0.02 NEAR storage deposit bound is an estimate and the callback panics if actual storage cost exceeds it.
- **Authorization gate:** observation, finalization, reorg, reservation, broadcast, confirmation and `reserve_entitlement` remain Rust-only. `allocate_entitlement` is callable only by the arena fixed at initialization, which must itself be trusted to call it only for computed settlement allocations; the contract does not verify that an amount belongs to a losing vault. Future adapters must authenticate player and DAO authority, bind destination and nonce, and validate source proofs. Fixture methods are self-call-only and must never be enabled in deployed builds.
- **Accounting:** unit and sandbox tests check actual-received amounts, exact normalization, attributed residue, duplicate receipt and debit handling, entitlement allocation, and exclusive liabilities across redemption, payout and disposal pools. The ledger view proves arithmetic only, not external solvency. A broadcast or timeout has no release path; confirmation compares the asset, custody, destination, amounts and transaction identity but cannot itself establish finality.
- **Reorg, dispute and storage:** `report_registry_dispute` lets anyone pull a registry-confirmed dispute, which pauses the asset and requires reconciliation while retaining liabilities. There is no unpause path and no automatic reversal. A dispute raised after units were minted or reserved does not claw anything back. Storage unregister is disabled to prevent a pending NEP-141 refund from being burned outside the ledger.
- **Remaining scope:** a live custody source for the deficit attestation, executing the late refund on the source chain (payout), DAO authorization and disposal, signing, authentication of the beneficiary or delegate for reservations, lifecycle integration and live testnet evidence remain outstanding. Do not archive the OpenSpec as completed on fixture results.
- **Suspension and resume:** the deficit signal is a trusted operator attestation, not proof of custody. `OwnerWithEvidence` (default) needs the owner and a later covering attestation. `Automatic` lets a later covering attestation lift a suspension with no human review, so a wrong or manipulated attestation can reopen minting and payouts while a shortfall remains: it trades safety for speed. `Disabled` blocks resume while selected, and the owner can change the policy at any time. Unresolved reservations are never lifted by resume. A reorg or deficit that follows a covering attestation at an earlier block than the incident is not detected; the operator must attest after the event.
- **Late deposits:** lock is judged at claim time through the arena view; a missing or failing view credits nothing. The beneficiary named in the event is a NEAR account, so the refund destination must be authenticated by the payout contract. Refund reservation requires the payout account and one yocto.
- **Refund address (D17):** the destination is chosen by the depositor at deposit time and attested by the operator, so a mistyped or contract-incapable address loses the refund and nothing corrects it after binding; the custody contract cannot check that it controls the address. Late units are tracked per bound address, so a payout cannot redirect them, but a compromised operator can attest any address for a receipt. Residue stays attributed to the beneficiary and is not refundable here.
- **Payout late refund (internal review, not an audit):** `request_late_refund` takes no destination from backing's point of view beyond what backing validates; the callback is `#[private]`, state is written only after the backing call succeeds, the failure path refunds the deposit, and the contract spends one yocto of its own balance per call (negligible, bounded by the caller's deposit). A caller can pick among addresses bound to its own deposits but cannot add one. Remaining risks: a wrong bound address is unrecoverable, and confirmation relies on the trusted operator's evidence.
