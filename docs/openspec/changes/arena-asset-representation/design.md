## Context

One independently reviewed change in the arena redesign. See [coordination](../../README.md) and [product specification](../../../product-spec.md).

## Goals / Non-Goals

Deliver backed asset representation and its acceptance scenarios without claiming unrelated work or unverified network support.

## Decisions

- Contract: [capability requirements](specs/arena-asset-representation/spec.md).
- Integration boundary: Define deposit and custody interface with lifecycle.
- Asset identity binds source chain/token, NEAR representation and custody domain.
- Late foreign deposits (owner decision): a deposit that arrives after lock never enters a snapshot or vault. Backing records it as unattributed and returns it to the original depositor after finality. Lifecycle owns the lock boundary; this change owns the recording and the refund path.
- Keep custody verifier, oracle, signer, operator and player authorization boundaries explicit. Fixtures do not prove live execution. DAO governance and provider/finality choices remain OPEN.

## Risks / Migration

Cross-chain operations must be idempotent, reconciled before retry and fail closed on missing evidence. Use isolated testnet deployments; legacy migration is out of scope.

## Receipt and liability boundary

One representation instance binds an immutable source chain, token, custody domain, source decimals and representation decimals. A receipt is uniquely identified by chain, transaction hash and log index; replay prevention must not depend on textual hash casing. Amounts are integer source units actually credited to custody, rather than the nominal transfer amount. Normalization must use the instance's fixed decimals, floor on downscaling, attribute any source residue, and reject overflow.

The receipt verifier is a trust boundary, not a claim that an operator's statement proves finality. The specific verifier, custody proof, finality/reorg policy and tax handling remain OPEN. A production path must fail closed until those decisions and authenticated evidence are in place. Test fixtures must be labeled and must not be usable as production evidence. A provisional or reorganized receipt cannot mint; a post-finality deficit must pause affected actions and preserve liabilities pending reconciliation.

Representation supply, allocated entitlements, payout/redemption reservations, DAO disposal reservations and unallocated attributed units are mutually exclusive liabilities over verified custody. Every transition must preserve `B = M + E + P + D + U`; nonrepresentable source residue is tracked separately. A reservation must remove the corresponding spendable liability before external execution, bind a unique operation identity, and remain reserved after a timeout or ambiguous callback. Restoration is permitted only after evidence establishes that no executable source-chain spend remains; neither a missing response nor a failed local callback is sufficient. Any disposition authority remains out of scope until DAO governance is designed.

The reference branch is not accepted as a verifier: its receipt method accepts caller-supplied decimal precision and its payout confirmation does not bind all evidence to the transaction and asset. Its failure handling also requires independent review for correct liability restoration. Implementations and tests must not treat those paths as proof of finalized custody or safe recovery.

Replay protection within one representation contract is insufficient if the same custody receipt can be credited by multiple representation contracts. Before enabling a verifier in production, the system must enforce a unique representation per source asset and custody domain or consume each receipt in a shared authoritative registry. That cross-instance mechanism is not established by local fixture tests.

## Testnet verifier decision

The user selected a trusted operator verifier for testnet. The operator is trusted to check and attest source-chain evidence; its attestation is not a cryptographic proof of Robinhood state. A live adapter must authenticate a separately configured verifier identity, bind its attestation to the immutable asset mapping and exact source receipt, and document operator powers, rotation and revocation. It must establish actual custody increase and beneficiary attribution rather than accepting a nominal amount. Unverified, contradictory or reorganized evidence must fail closed. Fixture self-calls do not authenticate this operator.

For testnet, use the source RPC `finalized` block as the minimum finality boundary. The operator must verify the successful transaction and receipt, canonical block hash and height, and that the receipt block is an ancestor of the finalized head; a numeric height alone is not sufficient. Check chain ID, token, custody, transaction/log identity, beneficiary attribution and actual custody increase under the chosen custody mechanism. Record the finalized head and source block evidence with the attestation. If the RPC is unavailable, does not support the tag, disagrees on canonicality, or evidence is incomplete, the receipt stays Pending. Do not substitute `latest`, a fixed confirmation count, or a nominal transfer amount. A disputed finalized receipt pauses affected actions and retains liabilities until independently reconciled; no automatic reversal or unpause is authorized.

Use a shared authoritative receipt-consumption registry for all representation contracts in the custody domain. The key is the source chain ID, binary transaction hash and log index; bind the immutable claim to the representation, asset mapping, beneficiary and attested amount. Authenticate the operator and permitted representation instances. Registry consumption must be durable and idempotent across callbacks, crashes and retries: an ambiguous callback cannot release the key or credit a second representation. Recovery may complete only the original bound claim; conflicting claims are rejected. The registry and representation contract must account for asynchronous NEAR calls, storage costs, authorization and operator rotation before a live mint path is enabled.

The finalized receipt attestation must identify the source chain, token and custody domain, representation account and mapping version, beneficiary and battle attribution, transaction hash and log index, receipt block number and hash, finalized head number and hash, actual received source units and the underlying evidence for attribution and custody balance reconciliation. Retain the source evidence or its durable reference and digest. The canonical binary `(chain_id, transaction_hash, log_index)` is the global replay key; representation identity and amount are immutable claim data, not part of that key. An absent beneficiary, unverified custody increase, failed transaction or mismatched asset does not establish a claim.

The verifier account, custody contract deployment, balance-attribution verification and deployment configuration still require implementation and review. The `finalized` rule and shared registry are testnet design choices, not proof that the configured endpoint or the implementations support them. Do not expose a live receipt/mint path until these are specified, enforced and verified. No mainnet trust or deployment is implied. See [Robinhood's network configuration](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/connecting/) and [Arbitrum's finality guidance](https://docs.arbitrum.io/build-decentralized-apps/troubleshooting-building).

For testnet deposit attribution, the selected custody mechanism is a dedicated source-chain custody contract that records each deposit. Its deposit operation must bind the depositor, token, custody domain, mapping version, beneficiary and battle; measure the actual balance increase around the transfer; emit a uniquely identifiable deposit event; and reject failed, zero or inconsistent receipts. The operator verifies the configured custody contract and matching finalized transaction/event as well as actual balance evidence. Direct transfers or donations outside that operation must never create a deposit claim. Balance-delta measurements are not by themselves proof against malicious or rebasing token behavior; token eligibility, adversarial execution and transfer-tax policy remain separate gates. The custody contract must not be deployed for live use until its withdrawal, reservation-bound authorization and recovery paths are implemented and reviewed.

## Identity contract with lifecycle

The mapping committed by a lifecycle community consists of source chain ID `46630`, normalized 20-byte source-token address, custody domain, NEAR representation account, source and representation decimals, and an immutable mapping version. A community may also commit an optional mainnet reference pair (`reference_chain_id` and a normalized 20-byte lowercase `reference_address`, both set or both unset), used by the audit to attest the mainnet token a testnet mock represents; it is part of the mapping, so changing it requires a new version and invalidates affected approval, and no two communities may share a reference pair. The representation account is its NEAR contract account, not a symbol or user-supplied alias. The configured registry is shared across all representations in that custody domain, and its owner configures the operator verifier and authorized representation accounts. The operator attestation and the registry claim bind the same mapping version and source-token/custody identity to each receipt; lifecycle must reject approvals and backed deposits that do not match its committed community mapping. Replacing any mapping field requires a new version and invalidates affected pre-lock approval. No locked snapshot may be amended by a later mapping or verifier change.

This is the intended integration contract, not a claim that the current lifecycle ABI already carries decimals, mapping version or the registry identity. The lifecycle ABI does now carry the optional reference pair (arena-lifecycle); backing code and tests were not changed and do not compare it. Implementing and testing those cross-contract checks remains separate work. Actual operator account IDs and custody addresses belong to reviewed testnet deployment configuration, not hard-coded defaults.

## Suspension, resume policy and late deposits

Suspension reasons are `reorg`, `deficit` and a count of unresolved reservations. Any reason sets `reconciliation_required`, which blocks claims, minting, `allocate_entitlement` and every new reservation; confirmations and no-spend releases stay allowed because they record a real debit or move a liability between buckets. Only a reorg also halts NEP-141 transfers. The deficit signal is `attest_custody_balance`, callable only by the owner-configured operator with one yocto: custody balance at a finalized source block, strictly increasing block numbers, compared with liabilities in source units (rounded up, plus attributed residue). Unresolved reservations are cleared only by confirmation or release, never by resume.

Resume policy is a stored enum (D14): `OwnerWithEvidence` (default), `Automatic`, `Disabled`. `Automatic` trades safety for speed. Changes are owner-only, one yocto, and emit `resume_policy_changed`.

Late deposits (D8, D15): `claim_receipt` asks the arena's existing `get_lifecycle_battle` view whether the claim's battle is locked. Locked means the units go to bucket U as a late balance for the beneficiary named in the deposit event, claimable as nothing else. An unknown battle or unavailable view credits nothing. Refund is reserved by the payout account through `reserve_late_refund` (U to P) and then follows `confirm_payout` or `release_payout`. Lock is judged when backing processes the claim, not when the source deposit was mined.

Refund address binding (D17, refines D15): the custody `deposit` takes a non-zero `refundAddress` (EVM) and emits it in `Deposited(uint256,address,address,uint64,uint64,address,string,uint256,uint256)`; the changed signature is a new event layout that the verifier decodes and the old layout fails to decode, so a deployment must commit a new mapping version for it. The verifier rejects a zero address and attests it; the registry claim carries it as immutable claim data (a different address is a conflicting claim); backing records it on the late deposit and tracks late units per `(beneficiary, refund address)`. `reserve_late` accepts only a destination that holds units bound at deposit, so neither another address nor another account can receive them; release restores the same pair. A claim without a refund address credits nothing. The NEAR beneficiary still names the account that owns the late balance; the refund address names where it goes.

Payout side (D17): `request_late_refund(backing, request_id, amount, refund_address)` on the payout contract is called by the beneficiary, asks backing for `reserve_late_refund` with one yocto from the payout contract's own balance, and on success records the payout through the same callback, signing, confirmation and release path as an entitlement payout. The destination is not free: backing accepts only an address bound at deposit, and confirmation evidence must name that recipient. A beneficiary with several deposits may choose among the addresses bound to its own deposits, never a new one.
